Maritime & shipping operations
- Iran's alternative-route squeeze on Saudi Arabia now extends beyond Hormuz itself: Yemen's Houthis captured the port of Mokha and the island of Mayun (Perim) at the mouth of the Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb Strait on Sep 10-11, extending their control over one of the world's key commercial shipping lanes (~12% of global goods transit Bab el-Mandeb). This directly threatens the Red Sea diversion route Saudi Arabia had been using as an alternative to Hormuz, reportedly forcing Riyadh to consider routing oil even further north via Suez or the Egyptian pipeline — a materially longer route for Asian clients. — Source: Washington Post / NPR / Al Jazeera, Sep 11-12, 2026

Gulf regional infrastructure
- Saudi Arabia shut down its East-West Crude Oil Pipeline — its main non-Hormuz export route, carrying roughly 5m bpd (4-5% of global oil supply) to the Red Sea port of Yanbu — after unclaimed drone strikes from Iraqi territory hit the pipeline on Sep 10, causing fires, pump-station damage, and injuries. Combined with the Houthi advance on Bab el-Mandeb (see Maritime), Saudi Arabia's two main alternatives to the blockaded Strait of Hormuz have both been disrupted within the same 48-hour window — a significant compounding of the region's export-logistics squeeze. — Source: Bloomberg / CNBC / CNN, Sep 10-11, 2026
